🍗 Sample "Non-Vegetarian" Daily Meal Plan

This structure applies to all age groups; simply increase portion sizes as the athlete gets older.

🍳

Breakfast (Focus: Complex Carbs + Protein)

  • 9–12 yrs: 1 cup oatmeal with berries and a hard-boiled egg.
  • 13–16 yrs: 2 cups oatmeal, 2 eggs, and a glass of milk.
  • 17–20 yrs: 2+ cups oatmeal with nuts/seeds, 3 eggs, and a side of turkey sausage.
🍎

Morning Snack (School/Training)

Greek yogurt with honey or a banana with peanut butter.

🌯

Lunch (Focus: Lean Protein + Sustained Energy)

The Build: A grilled chicken breast or lean beef wrap with whole-wheat tortilla, hummus, spinach, peppers, and a side of fruit.

🥨

Pre-Workout Snack (30–60 mins before)

Easy-to-digest carbs: A handful of pretzels, an apple, or a granola bar.

🥩

Dinner (Focus: Recovery & Growth)

  • Protein: Grilled salmon, lean steak, or roasted chicken.
  • Carbs: Sweet potato, brown rice, or quinoa.
  • Vegetables: Broccoli, asparagus, or a large mixed salad.
Note: 13–18-year-olds should double the portion of starchy carbohydrates if training is high-intensity.
🥛

Evening/Recovery Snack (If needed)

Cottage cheese (casein protein is excellent for overnight recovery) or a chocolate milk (the "gold standard" for post-workout recovery).

⚙️ Key Nutrient Priorities & Tips

🩸

Key Nutrient Priorities for Young Athletes

  • Iron (Ages 13–18): Essential for oxygen transport. Increase intake of red meat, dark leafy greens, and fortified cereals.
  • Calcium/Vitamin D: Critical for bone density during growth spurts. Ensure daily dairy or fortified alternatives.
  • Hydration: The most overlooked performance factor.
    Simple Rule: If the urine is darker than pale lemonade, they are dehydrated.
    • 9–12 yrs: Carry a 20oz water bottle; aim for 2–3 per day.
    • 13–20 yrs: Aim for 3–4 liters of water daily, plus electrolytes if sweating heavily.
💡

Practical Tips for Parents

  • The "Post-Practice Window": Within 30 minutes of finishing practice, the athlete should eat a combination of protein and carbs (e.g., a turkey sandwich or a protein smoothie) to stop muscle breakdown.
  • Avoid "Empty" Fuel: Minimize soda, energy drinks, and heavily processed sugary snacks. They cause "crashes" during games.
  • Individualize: Every child’s metabolism is different. If they are losing weight or feeling lethargic, increase their complex carbohydrate intake (e.g., more brown rice or sweet potatoes).

⚡ Essential Rules for All Ages

🚫 The "No-Go" List

Minimize processed sugars and ultra-processed foods. These lead to "energy crashes" mid-game. Badminton requires consistent focus; soda and candy cause blood sugar spikes followed by drops.

💧 Hydration Hierarchy

  • Under 60 mins of play: Water only.
  • Over 60 mins: Electrolyte-enhanced water (low sugar).

🍳 Consistency

Never skip breakfast. A badminton player's brain needs glucose from breakfast to coordinate the quick footwork required on court.

🎒 Travel Nutrition (Go-Bag)

Tournament days are unpredictable. Always pack:

  • 🍌 Bananas
  • 🥪 Nut-butter sandwiches
  • 🍫 Energy bars (<10g sugar)
  • 🚰 Water bottle
